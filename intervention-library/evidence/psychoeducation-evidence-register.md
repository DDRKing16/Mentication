# Mentication Psychoeducation Evidence Register

Version: 2026-09-06 | Scope: 17 flagship interventions | Next scheduled review: September 2027

Grades describe the fit between the claim and the cited evidence, not product efficacy: A = direct evidence for the named general technique; B = evidence for the main mechanism or a broader intervention; C = mechanism-adjacent or exact branded sequence not directly tested.

## 1. Box Breathing

**User-facing psychoeducation:** Slow, structured breathing can support autonomic regulation and increase heart-rate variability. Evidence is stronger for slow-paced breathing generally than for the exact 4-4-4-4 pattern.

**Evidence fit:** A for slow-paced breathing; C for the exact branded pattern

**Limits and safety:** Do not imply that the exact four-part ratio is superior. Stop or remove holds if dizziness, air hunger or respiratory discomfort occurs.

**Sources:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35623448/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35623448/)

**Review status:** Approved for provisional product use with the stated limitations. Reviewed 2026-09-06; next review due 2027-09-06 or sooner if the mechanism or copy changes.

## 2. Progressive Muscle Relaxation

**User-facing psychoeducation:** Progressive muscle relaxation has evidence for reducing stress and anxiety in adults. The useful mechanism is learning the contrast between tension and release, not forcing every muscle to relax.

**Evidence fit:** A for PMR generally; C for this interaction system

**Limits and safety:** Evidence covers varied PMR protocols and populations. Avoid painful or injured areas and use release-only when tensing is unsuitable.

**Sources:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38322293/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38322293/)

**Review status:** Approved for provisional product use with the stated limitations. Reviewed 2026-09-06; next review due 2027-09-06 or sooner if the mechanism or copy changes.

## 3. 5-4-3-2-1 Grounding

**User-facing psychoeducation:** Grounding uses present sensory information to redirect attention toward the here and now. The exact 5-4-3-2-1 sequence is widely used, but direct efficacy research on that specific sequence remains limited.

**Evidence fit:** C: mechanism-informed; exact sequence under-studied

**Limits and safety:** The review notes that grounding has lacked agreed definitions and efficacy studies. Never present this as proven treatment for dissociation or trauma.

**Sources:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40575850/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40575850/)

**Review status:** Approved for provisional product use with the stated limitations. Reviewed 2026-09-06; next review due 2027-09-06 or sooner if the mechanism or copy changes.

## 4. Thought or Fact

**User-facing psychoeducation:** Cognitive approaches can reduce anxiety and depression by helping people examine interpretations and generate more balanced alternatives. This is a brief sorting tool, not a diagnostic test or a verdict on whether a person is right.

**Evidence fit:** B for CBT/cognitive change; C for this brief standalone sorter

**Limits and safety:** Do not use to challenge trauma memories, abuse disclosures, delusional or paranoid beliefs, or immediate danger. Evidence is for broader CBT packages, not this screen sequence alone.

**Sources:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27717254/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27717254/), [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38228727/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38228727/)

**Review status:** Approved for provisional product use with the stated limitations. Reviewed 2026-09-06; next review due 2027-09-06 or sooner if the mechanism or copy changes.

## 5. Urge Surfing

**User-facing psychoeducation:** Mindfulness practice may weaken the link between craving and action even when the urge does not immediately disappear. The goal is a more available choice, not a promise that every urge will pass within a fixed time.

**Evidence fit:** B for mindfulness and craving-response decoupling; C for this brief wave interaction

**Limits and safety:** Results for brief app-based mindfulness are mixed. This is not suitable when an urge involves immediate danger, overdose risk, self-harm or harm to others.

**Sources:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23265088/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23265088/), [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29917096/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29917096/)

## 6. Then What?

**User-facing psychoeducation:** Cognitive change and perspective-taking can reduce emotional impact by changing how a situation is understood. This pathway tests coping beyond the worst frame; it is not reassurance and does not establish what will happen.

**Evidence fit:** B for cognitive change/perspective-taking; C for this bounded standalone sequence

**Limits and safety:** Not for immediate danger, acute panic, trauma re-experiencing, impaired reality testing or repetitive certainty-seeking. Broader CBT evidence does not prove the exact sequence.

**Sources:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22582737/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22582737/), [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38228727/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38228727/)

## 7. Ignition Point

**User-facing psychoeducation:** Behavioural activation works by increasing meaningful contact between a person and their environment. A small action can be worthwhile before mood changes, so success is engagement with value or reward rather than instant happiness.

**Evidence fit:** A for behavioural activation packages; C for the four-pathway branded selector

**Limits and safety:** Evidence is mainly from depression treatment protocols delivered over time. One micro-action should not be described as treatment or guaranteed mood improvement.

**Sources:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37068380/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37068380/), [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37227760/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37227760/)

**Review status:** Approved for provisional product use with the stated limitations. Reviewed 2026-09-06; next review due 2027-09-06 or sooner if the mechanism or copy changes.

## 8. The Happy Bump

**User-facing psychoeducation:** Behavioural activation can improve low mood by increasing contact with rewarding, meaningful and mastery-building activity before motivation arrives. Movement, social connection, positive reflection and values-led action are offered as accessible supports, not guaranteed mood treatments.

**Evidence fit:** B+ for behavioural activation; mixed and component-specific evidence for the combined sequence

**Limits and safety:** The combined branded sequence has not been independently tested. Movement must remain safe and capacity-matched; music, hydration and social contact do not guarantee a mood shift. The six wellbeing areas are evidence-informed domains, not a definitive universal ranking.

**Sources:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37068380/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37068380/), [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37227760/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37227760/)

**Review status:** Approved for provisional product use with the stated limitations. Reviewed 2026-09-06; next review due 2027-09-06 or sooner if the mechanism or copy changes.

## 9. Change the Scene

**User-facing psychoeducation:** Stable contexts can cue automatic behaviour, while a context change can interrupt those cues and create a window for a different choice. Evidence supports the broader context-discontinuity principle, not this exact short intervention.

**Evidence fit:** C: mechanism-adjacent context research

**Limits and safety:** The cited context-discontinuity evidence concerns longer-term habit contexts, not momentary mood shifts. Keep claims modest and never direct users into unsafe environments.

**Sources:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27120333/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27120333/)

**Review status:** Approved for provisional product use with the stated limitations. Reviewed 2026-09-06; next review due 2027-09-06 or sooner if the mechanism or copy changes.

## 10. Test the Prediction

**User-facing psychoeducation:** Behavioural experiments can help people compare a prediction with observable experience. Evidence suggests benefit within anxiety treatment, but results must be read fairly and should never be designed to prove the user wrong.

**Evidence fit:** B for behavioural experiments in anxiety treatment; C for this standalone digital path

**Limits and safety:** The review found methodological limitations and studied behavioural experiments inside treatment. Never create high-stakes, unsafe or boundary-violating tests.

**Sources:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20381224/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20381224/)

**Review status:** Approved for provisional product use with the stated limitations. Reviewed 2026-09-06; next review due 2027-09-06 or sooner if the mechanism or copy changes.

## 11. Open Channel

**User-facing psychoeducation:** Interventions that increase access to people or address barriers to connection can improve social contact and perceived connection. One message is only a bridge, not a guaranteed repair or cure for loneliness.

**Evidence fit:** B for broader social-connection interventions; C for a single digital re-entry prompt

**Limits and safety:** Intervention effects vary and much evidence concerns multi-session programmes. Do not use for abuse, coercion, unsafe former partners or when no contact has been requested.

**Sources:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34796368/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34796368/), [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34339939/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34339939/)

## 12. Countermove

**User-facing psychoeducation:** Behavioural activation increases meaningful interaction with the environment, and specific action plans can help close the gap between intention and behaviour. A small directional move can count before mood changes.

**Evidence fit:** B for behavioural activation and implementation intentions; C for the graded trajectory interface

**Limits and safety:** Evidence comes from broader interventions, not the 5/20/45-degree metaphor. Do not oppose behaviour that is protective, restorative or required for safety.

## 13. Pulse Shift

**User-facing psychoeducation:** A single bout of exercise is associated on average with modest improvements in mood and anxiety, but responses vary widely. Here the aim is simply to make a little more movement available, not to prescribe intensity or promise a mood lift.

**Evidence fit:** B for acute exercise affect; C for micro-dose graded activation

**Limits and safety:** The meta-analysis found substantial heterogeneity and typically studied exercise bouts, not tiny movements. This is not medical or exercise advice.

**Sources:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38787545/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38787545/)

**Review status:** Approved for provisional product use with the stated limitations. Reviewed 2026-09-06; next review due 2027-09-06 or sooner if the mechanism or copy changes.

## 14. Reroute

**User-facing psychoeducation:** Distraction can reduce emotional impact in some contexts, but its usefulness depends on the emotion, timing and purpose. Reroute is a deliberate short-term shift of attention, not suppression or a claim that the problem no longer matters.

**Evidence fit:** C: mechanism-informed attentional deployment

**Limits and safety:** The meta-analysis found strategy and context differences; attentional deployment was not uniformly effective. Do not use when urgent safety or practical action is required.

**Sources:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22582737/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22582737/)

**Review status:** Approved for provisional product use with the stated limitations. Reviewed 2026-09-06; next review due 2027-09-06 or sooner if the mechanism or copy changes.

## 15. Next Easiest Step

**User-facing psychoeducation:** Specific action plans can improve goal attainment by linking intention to a concrete response. The useful target is a visible next behaviour, not a perfect plan or a judgment about motivation.

**Evidence fit:** B for implementation intentions; C for task-compression micro-intervention

**Limits and safety:** If-then planning evidence does not establish an optimal 20-second start rule. Keep the action user-editable and do not score productivity.

**Sources:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25965276/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25965276/)

## 16. Signal Lock

**User-facing psychoeducation:** Reducing interruption costs and using deliberate time-management strategies can support task accuracy, resumption and wellbeing. Evidence does not show that one branded timer length is universally best.

**Evidence fit:** B for interruption mitigation/time management; C for the exact sprint engine

**Limits and safety:** Much interruption evidence is laboratory-based and time-management evidence is correlational or heterogeneous. Do not claim the timer treats attention disorders.

**Sources:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34273814/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34273814/), [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33428644/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33428644/)

## 17. Tomorrow Parking Lot

**User-facing psychoeducation:** In one small sleep-laboratory study, writing a specific bedtime to-do list was associated with faster sleep onset than writing about completed activities. This supports offloading as a useful option, not a guarantee of sleep.

**Evidence fit:** B-: one small randomised laboratory study plus cognitive-offloading rationale

**Limits and safety:** The study included 57 healthy young adults and used five minutes of writing. Do not promise sleep onset or generalise to clinical insomnia without caution.

**Sources:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29058942/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29058942/)

**Review status:** Approved for provisional product use with the stated limitations. Reviewed 2026-09-06; next review due 2027-09-06 or sooner if the mechanism or copy changes.

## 18. Night Channel

**User-facing psychoeducation:** Music may improve subjective sleep quality for some adults, while effects on objective sleep and other audio formats are less certain. Choose content that feels settling; Night Channel does not guarantee sleep.

**Evidence fit:** B for music listening; C/insufficient for podcasts, audiobooks and the branded channel system

**Limits and safety:** Subjective music findings are more positive than objective measures, and evidence for general-population sleep is mixed. External content quality and autoplay are outside Mentication's control.

**Sources:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36000763/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36000763/), [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33896299/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33896299/)
