EXTENDED EXHALE
A few long sighs signal safety to your nervous system — the fastest way to step the brakes.

  1. Settle
     Soften your shoulders. We’ll take a few extended sighs — no effort, just follow along.

  2. Double inhale, long exhale
     In through the nose, then one short sip more at the top. Let it all go through the mouth with a soft sigh.
     [Pace: Breathe in 3s  -  A little more 1s  -  Long sigh out 6s]

  3. Again, gently
     Let the exhale be longer than the inhale. That’s the whole secret.
     [Pace: Breathe in 3s  -  A little more 1s  -  Long sigh out 6s]

  4. Pause
     Notice any small softening — a little more room in the chest.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

CYCLIC SIGHING
Repeating the sigh is one of the most effective ways to quickly calm the body.

  1. Begin the cycle
     Each round: two inhales, one long sigh out.

  2. Round one
     [Pace: In 2s  -  More 1s  -  Sigh out 6s]

  3. Round two
     [Pace: In 2s  -  More 1s  -  Sigh out 6s]

  4. Round three
     [Pace: In 2s  -  More 1s  -  Sigh out 6s]

  5. Rest
     Let the breath settle on its own.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

BOX BREATHING
A steady square of breath steadies a racing heart and brings you back to the room.

  1. Find your seat
     Feet on the floor, hands soft. We’ll breathe a calm square — in, hold, out, hold.

  2. 4 · 4 · 4 · 4
     Follow the circle. If you lose count, just rejoin.
     [Pace: Breathe in 4s  -  Hold 4s  -  Breathe out 4s  -  Hold 4s]

  3. Keep the square
     Let each side be smooth and unhurried.
     [Pace: Breathe in 4s  -  Hold 4s  -  Breathe out 4s  -  Hold 4s]

  4. Rest
     Let the breath find its own rhythm.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

SOFT BELLY BREATHING
Slow, even breathing at around six a minute settles your whole system.

  1. Soften the belly
     Let your belly be loose. About five seconds in, five out.

  2. Even in, even out
     Match inhale to exhale. Smooth, slow, easy.
     [Pace: Breathe in 5s  -  Breathe out 5s]

  3. Stay with it
     Mind wandering is fine — come back to the count.
     [Pace: Breathe in 5s  -  Breathe out 5s]

  4. Rest
     Let go of the count and rest in the stillness.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

4-7-8 BREATH
A longer hold and exhale strongly cues the calming branch of your nervous system.

  1. Tongue to ridge
     Rest the tongue just behind your front teeth. Exhale fully first.

  2. In 4 · Hold 7 · Out 8
     Inhale through the nose for 4, hold for 7, exhale through the mouth for 8.
     [Pace: Breathe in 4s  -  Hold 7s  -  Breathe out 8s]

  3. Again
     Keep the count steady. The long exhale does the work.
     [Pace: Breathe in 4s  -  Hold 7s  -  Breathe out 8s]

  4. Settle
     Let the breath return to normal.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

ALTERNATE NOSTRIL
Balancing the two sides of the breath brings a deeply settled, clear-headed calm.

  1. Right hand ready
     Thumb on right nostril, ring finger on left. We’ll alternate sides.

  2. Close right, inhale left
     Close the right nostril, inhale through the left.
     [Pace: In (left) 4s]

  3. Switch, exhale right
     Close left, open right, exhale. Then inhale right.
     [Pace: Out (right) 4s  -  In (right) 4s]

  4. Switch, exhale left
     Close right, open left, exhale. That’s one round — continue gently.
     [Pace: Out (left) 4s]

  5. A few more rounds
     Keep alternating at your own easy pace.

  6. Finish
     Rest both hands. Notice the balance.

COUNT TO TEN
Counting each exhale up to ten is a classic way to quiet a busy mind.

  1. Count each exhale
     Exhale, count ‘one’. Next exhale, ‘two’… up to ten, then restart.

  2. Stay with the count
     If you lose track, just start again at one. No judgement.

  3. Notice
     Did the mind quieten a little?

5-4-3-2-1 GROUNDING
Anchoring your senses pulls you out of the storm and back into the present.

  1. 5 things you see
     Name five things you can see. Ordinary is perfect.

  2. 4 you can touch
     Four things you can feel — fabric, chair, floor.

  3. 3 you hear
     Three sounds, near or far.

  4. 2 you smell · 1 you taste
     Two scents, one taste. You’re here, fully.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

ORIENTING SCAN
When the alarm is loud, your brain needs proof you’re safe — here, now.

  1. Look around
     Slowly turn your head. Let your eyes find the room’s edges.

  2. Name what you see
     Three things you can see. Rest your eyes on each.

  3. Where you are
     Say: I am here. This room. This moment. I am safe right now.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

SENSORY SOOTHING
One gentle, comforting input to each sense calms an overloaded system.

  1. One soothing sight
     Soft light, a plant, the sky. Rest your eyes there.

  2. One comforting touch
     Something soft or warm against your skin.

  3. One gentle sound
     A low steady sound, or your own slow breath.

  4. Settle
     Let the senses hold you for a moment.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

NAME THREE
A lighter version of grounding when five senses feels like too much.

  1. Three you see
     Name three objects around you, out loud or in your mind.

  2. Three sounds
     Name three sounds you can hear.

  3. Three you feel
     Three points of contact with your body.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

COLOUR HUNT
Searching for a colour gives an overloaded mind a single, calming task.

  1. Pick a colour
     Choose one — say, blue.

  2. Find five
     Spot five things that colour around you.

  3. Now another colour
     Pick a new colour, find three more.

  4. Settle
     Notice you’re a little more here.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

TEXTURE TOUR
Touch is a fast, direct route back to the body.

  1. Find five textures
     Fabric, wood, skin, metal, paper — touch each.

  2. Describe each
     Smooth, rough, cool, warm, soft, ridged.

  3. Settle
     Let the hands rest, the world steady.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

TEMPERATURE ANCHOR
Noticing temperature sharply brings you into the body.

  1. Cool on the hands
     Notice the air on your hands, or touch something cool.

  2. Warm on the chest
     Place a warm hand on your chest, feel the contrast.

  3. Rest
     Notice the present temperature, here, now.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

======================================================================
COGNITIVE DEFUSION
======================================================================

THOUGHT LABELING
Naming a thought as just a thought loosens its grip.

  1. Notice the thought
     Let one arrive. Don’t push it away.

  2. Name it
     ‘A worry.’ ‘A memory.’ ‘A what-if.’ Just the label.

  3. Let it pass
     Imagine it as a leaf on water, drifting by.

  4. Come back
     Another may come — label that one too.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

LEAVES ON A STREAM
Placing each thought on a floating leaf is a classic defusion practice.

  1. Picture a stream
     Slow water, leaves drifting past.

  2. Each thought on a leaf
     Place a thought on a leaf, watch it float away.

  3. Don’t follow
     Just watch. The stream keeps moving.

  4. Rest by the bank
     You’re the watcher, not the thoughts.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

NAME THE STORY
‘I’m having the story that…’ instantly creates distance from a thought.

  1. Catch the thought
     Notice what the mind is telling you.

  2. Prefix it
     Say: ‘I’m having the story that…’ and finish it.

  3. Notice the distance
     It’s a story, not a fact.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

THANK YOUR MIND
A wry ‘thanks, mind’ defuses a thought without fighting it.

  1. Notice the chatter
     There it goes again.

  2. Thank it
     ‘Thanks, mind, for that thought.’ Lightly.

  3. Let it be
     No argument. Just a nod.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

SING THE THOUGHT
Singing a worry to a silly tune strips it of its seriousness.

  1. Say the thought
     State the worry in one sentence.

  2. Sing it
     To ‘Happy Birthday’ or a jingle. Three times.

  3. Notice
     Does it still feel as true? As heavy?

----------------------------------------------------------------------

REPEAT UNTIL MEANINGLESS
Saying a sticky word for 30 seconds makes it lose all meaning.

  1. Pick the word
     The one word that keeps stabbing — ‘failure’, ‘alone’, etc.

  2. Say it for 30 seconds
     Out loud or silently, steady and quick.

  3. Notice it empty out
     Just sounds now. No sting.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

I’M NOTICING THAT…
Adding ‘I notice the thought that…’ adds a layer of observer distance.

  1. Catch it
     What’s the mind saying right now?

  2. Add the prefix
     ‘I notice I’m having the thought that…’

  3. Step back
     You’re the noticer, not the thought.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

THOUGHT AS CLOUD
Watching thoughts drift like clouds teaches the mind they pass on their own.

  1. Look up, in your mind
     A wide sky. Thoughts are clouds.

  2. Let them drift
     Each thought a cloud, passing by. No need to grab.

  3. The sky stays
     You’re the sky, not the clouds.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

THOUGHT OR FACT?
Asking whether a thought is a fact or a guess loosens certainty.

  1. State the thought
     In one clear sentence.

  2. Is it a fact?
     What’s the evidence? What’s the evidence against?

  3. Is it a guess?
     Could it be a story dressed as certainty?

----------------------------------------------------------------------

VALUES CHECK
Reconnecting to what matters puts a worry in perspective.

  1. What matters to you?
     Name one thing you care about deeply.

  2. Does this thought serve it?
     Does dwelling on this move you toward or away from it?

  3. One small step
     Toward your value, what’s one tiny thing you could do?

----------------------------------------------------------------------

THEN WHAT?
Following a fear to its end usually reveals you’d cope.

  1. State the fear
     The worst case, in one sentence.

  2. Then what?
     If that happened… then what? And then what?

  3. Could you cope?
     Honestly — what would you do to get through?

  4. Soften
     The edge comes off when you walk to the end.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

WORRY POSTPONEMENT
Parking worries for a set time shrinks their hold on the day.

  1. Set a worry time
     Pick 15 minutes later today for worries.

  2. Park this one
     Tell yourself: ‘I’ll worry about this then, not now.’

  3. Return to now
     Come back to what’s in front of you.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

POSSIBILITY VS PROBABILITY
Separating what could happen from what probably will calibrates fear.

  1. What’s the fear?
     The scary outcome, one sentence.

  2. Is it possible?
     Yes — but how likely, really, from 0–100%?

  3. What’s more likely?
     Name a more probable outcome.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

NAME THE CRITIC
Giving the harsh inner voice a name shrinks its authority.

  1. Hear the voice
     What’s it saying right now?

  2. Give it a name
     A silly or plain name. ‘The Manager’, ‘Brenda’.

     ‘I will work on ____.’ One thing.

  2. State the why
     One reason it matters, however small.

  3. Begin
     Carry that intention into the first move.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

ONE THING ONLY
Closing everything but one task removes the main focus killer.

  1. Close the rest
     Tabs, apps, notifications. One thing stays open.

  2. Phone face-down
     Out of sight, out of mind.

  3. Begin the one
     Only this, until the next break.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

ONE FOCUS BLOCK
A short, defined focus block makes starting feel safe and bounded.

  1. Set a block
     Twenty-five minutes, or whatever you can give.

  2. Start the timer
     When it runs, you focus. When it ends, you rest.

  3. Begin
     The timer makes it a game, not a threat.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

ATTENTION ANCHOR
A single object to return to trains the focus muscle gently.

  1. Pick an anchor
     A dot, a candle, a pen. One small thing at arm’s length.

  2. Rest your eyes there
     When the mind wanders, come back. No judgement.

  3. Carry that into the task
     The same gentle return, now with your work.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

BREATH BEFORE YOU BEGIN
A few settling breaths clear the noise right before you start.

  1. Sit, settle
     Feet down, shoulders soft.

  2. Six even breaths
     [Pace: In 4s  -  Out 5s]

  3. Open your eyes and begin
     Into the task, steady.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

MOVE THEN SIT
A brief body mobilisation wakes the alertness system before focus.

  1. Stand
     Quick shake of arms and legs.

  2. Brisk in place
     Thirty seconds, a little breathless.

  3. Sit and begin
     Alertness borrowed from the body.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

FIVE-MINUTE RULE
Permission to stop after five minutes dissolves the dread of starting.

  1. Five minutes only
     You can stop after five, guilt-free.

  2. Start the timer
     Begin now, before you feel ready.

  3. Decide at five
     Stop or keep going — either is a win.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

IF-THEN PLAN
‘If X, then I’ll do Y’ pre-loads the action so the brain follows through.

  1. Name the trigger
     When / where will you start?

  2. Name the action
     Exactly what you’ll do then.

  3. Say it
     ‘When ___, I will ___.’

----------------------------------------------------------------------

THE NEXT PHYSICAL STEP
Naming the literal next physical move ends paralysis by abstraction.

  1. Shrink the task
     Not the outcome — the next physical action.

  2. Name it
     ‘Open the doc.’ ‘Pick up the brush.’ One move.

  3. Do it
     That one move, now.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

RESET BEFORE YOU START
A quick sigh and orientation clears the residue of the last thing.

  1. One long sigh
     [Pace: In 3s  -  Sigh out 7s]

  2. Look around
     Let your eyes find the room.

  3. Turn to the task
     Fresh, not carrying the last thing.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

QUIET THE INPUTS
Silencing notifications and tabs is the fastest real focus gain.

  1. Phone silent
     Face-down or in another room.

  2. Close extra tabs
     Anything not for this task.

  3. Begin
     Less in, more focus.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

BREAK IT DOWN
Splitting a task into micro-steps makes the first step doable.

  1. Name the task
     The whole thing, one phrase.

  2. Three micro-steps
     The smallest first three moves.

  3. Start step one
     Only step one, now.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

SMALL REWARD AFTER
A small reward waiting after the block gives the brain a reason to start.

  1. Pick a reward
     Something small and real — a tea, a walk, a song.

  2. Earn it
     After this block, you get it.

  3. Begin
     The reward is closer than it feels.

======================================================================

WIND-DOWN BODY SCAN
A slow downward sweep tells the body it’s safe to let go for the night.

  1. Settle in
     Lie down, or sit back. Let the room hold you. Nothing to do now but rest.

  2. Feet & legs
     Soften your feet, your calves, your thighs. Let them grow heavy.

  3. Belly & breath
     Rest attention on the slow rise and fall. No counting — just riding it.

  4. Shoulders & jaw
     Let the shoulders melt. Let the jaw slacken. Nothing to hold up.

  5. Whole body
     One slow breath for the whole body, then let it all sink.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

BODY ROTATION
Moving attention quickly through the body in sequence quiets the mind into sleep.

  1. Right side
     Thumb, fingers, palm, wrist, elbow, shoulder — quick, light touches of attention.

  2. Left side
     The same on the left. Don’t linger — just pass through.

  3. Centre & face
     Lips, tongue, eyes, forehead. Soft, soft, soft.

  4. Whole body at once
     Hold the whole body in one wide attention. Let it rest.

EXHALE COUNTDOWN
Counting backwards on the exhale gives a looping mind a single, sleepy task.

  1. Count down from 60
     Each exhale, one number down. If you lose count, start again at 60.

  2. Keep going
     Slower each time. No effort — just the count.

  3. If you reach zero
     Start again. Or let the counting fade and rest.

  1. In 4, out 7

  2. Stay with it

  3. Even slower

  4. Drift
     Let it fade. Let sleep come if it will.

======================================================================
CONNECTION / SOCIAL
======================================================================

REACH OUT NOW
A single small reach interrupts the withdrawal that deepens low mood.

  1. Who comes to mind?
     The person most likely to make the next ten minutes a little easier.

  2. Keep it small
     A text, a call, a meme, a 'how are you'. No big conversation required.

  3. Send it
     One line. Then let it go.

  4. That counts
     You reached. Connection is the whole thing.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

SEND A VOICE NOTE
Your voice, sent lightly, is connection without the weight of a full call.

  1. Pick someone easy
     Someone you're comfortable with.

  2. Hit record
     Say hello, share something, tell them about your day, ask how they are.

  3. Send it
     Short is perfect. No pressure to talk long.

  4. Done
     Your voice, out in the world. That's connection.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

PET CONNECTION
A few minutes of real contact with an animal eases loneliness and stress.

  1. Find your pet
     Sit beside them, or call them over.

  2. Slow pats
     Long, slow strokes. Notice the texture of their fur.

  3. Their warmth
     Feel their breathing, their weight against you.

  4. Talk to them
     A few quiet words. They know your voice.

  5. Stay
     A few minutes of nothing to do but this.

  6. Notice
     A little less alone.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

BE AROUND PEOPLE
Gentle human presence, with no talking required, softens isolation.

  1. Go where people are
     A café, library, park, common area. Somewhere with gentle human presence.

  2. No talking required
     You're just near. That's enough.

  3. Settle
     Find a spot. Sit.

  4. Let the presence in
     Voices, movement, life around you.

  5. You're not alone in this
     Just being near can ease the ache.

======================================================================
EMOTION REGULATION
======================================================================

NAME WHAT YOU'RE FEELING
Putting a word on a feeling is a small act that reliably loosens its grip.

  1. Find the closest word
     Anxious, sad, angry, numb, hurt, ashamed, tense, flat. Whatever fits best.

  2. How strong?
     One to ten, roughly.

  3. What touched it off?
     If you can see it, name it. If not, that's fine too.

  4. Say it out loud
     Sometimes naming it shrinks it a notch.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT DOES THIS EMOTION NEED?
Asking what a feeling needs turns overwhelm into a next step.

  1. You named it
     Now: what would help most right now?

  2. Pick one
     Space, comfort, action, connection, reassurance, movement, expression, rest, clarity.

  3. Let it point you
     Whatever you chose — that's the thread to follow next.

  4. No wrong answer
     Just information about what you need.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

URGE SURFING
Riding an urge without acting lets it lose height on its own.

  1. Notice the urge
     Checking, reacting, messaging, avoiding, quitting, snapping. Name it quietly.

  2. Where in the body?
     Where do you feel it pulling?

  3. Rate it
     How strong, one to ten.

  4. Imagine a wave
     Rising, peaking, falling. Urges do that.

  5. Don't act yet
     Just ride it. It will lose height.

  6. Re-rate
     A little lower, maybe? Or the same — that's okay too.

  7. If it's still high
     Ride another wave. You don't have to act on it.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

RIDE THE WAVE
Staying with an emotion while it shifts teaches that it won't last forever.

  1. Find where it sits
     The emotion, somewhere in the body.

  2. How intense?
     Just notice. No need to change it.

  3. Stay with it
     Not fixing. Not fleeing. Just here with it.

  4. Watch it shift
     Emotions move like weather. Notice what changes.

  5. You're the space around it
     Not the feeling itself.

  6. Rest
     It can be here, and you can be okay.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

DON'T SEND IT YET
A short pause before reacting stops the message you can't take back.

  1. Don't send it yet
     Put the phone down. Leave the draft.

  2. Step away
     A short break. Two, five, or ten minutes.

  3. Breathe it down
     Long, slow exhales.
     [Pace: In 4s  -  Long out 6s]

  4. Again
     Let the exhale be longer each time.
     [Pace: In 4s  -  Long out 6s]

  5. When the timer ends
     Decide then. Not now. You don't have to figure out what to say.

COGNITIVE DEFUSION
======================================================================

CHECK THE BASICS
Sometimes the state is just a body asking for something simple.

  1. Quick scan
     Thirsty? Hungry? Too hot or cold? Sitting too long? Need air, daylight, the bathroom, less noise, less screen, sleep?

  2. One thing jumping out?
     Trust the first one.

  3. Tend to it
     Water, a snack, a stretch, fresh air, a quieter spot, a screen break. One small thing.

  4. Then see
     Sometimes the state was just a need in disguise.
