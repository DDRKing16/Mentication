# Elite Standards Pass 02 — PMR and Then What?

This pass completes the priority safety and sequence amendments identified in the intervention audit.

# Progressive Muscle Relaxation

## Contract

**Primary goal:** Calm  
**Primary design system:** Interactive Body Mapping  
**Intended state:** Noticeable physical tension, bracing or difficulty distinguishing tension from release.  
**Intended change:** Increase awareness of muscular contrast and support deliberate release without requiring total relaxation.  
**Core mechanism:** Brief, comfortable tension followed by release and attention to the contrast between the two states.  
**Signature material:** A translucent body field with localised light.  
**Motion verb:** Gather → release → soften.

## Purpose

PMR guides someone through comfortably tensing and releasing selected muscle groups so the contrast becomes easier to notice. It is intended for ordinary physical tension or stress when tensing is comfortable and safe; the user can skip any area or use a release-only route. Research suggests PMR can reduce stress and anxiety for some adults, but responses vary and the aim of one session is noticing and softening—not achieving complete relaxation.

## Revised sequence

~~~
HOME + CALM
↓
COMFORT AND SUITABILITY CHECK
↓
CHOOSE RELEASE ONLY · SHORT · FULL
↓
SETTLE INTO A SUPPORTED POSITION
↓
NOTICE THE STARTING BODY STATE
↓
SELECT OR BEGIN FIRST AREA
↓
GENTLY TENSE, OR IMAGINE GATHERING
↓
RELEASE
↓
NOTICE THE CONTRAST
↓
CONTINUE · SKIP · SLOW DOWN · STOP
↓
WHOLE-BODY SOFTENING
↓
WHERE IS TENSION NOW?
↓
FINISH OR SAVE PREFERRED ROUTE
~~~

## Suitability check

Opening question:

**“Is there any area that feels painful, injured or unsafe to tense today?”**

Routes:

- **No** → tension-and-release route is available.

- **Yes** → mark areas to skip, use release-only guidance, or choose another intervention.

- **Unsure or feeling medically unwell** → do not instruct active tensing; offer release-only guidance or exit.

No instruction should encourage pushing through pain, holding the breath, maximal contraction or tensing an area that feels unsafe. The user can change the skip map at any time.

## Adaptation levels

### Immediate — One Release

Approximately 30–45 seconds. Choose one comfortable area, apply a gentle contrast if appropriate, release and notice.

### Standard — Three Zones

Hands and arms → shoulders and face → legs and feet, with a whole-body release. Areas may be skipped or reordered.

### Supported — Full Body

Six-area sequence with slower pacing, more positioning support, optional narration and longer pauses.

### Release Only

No active contraction. The user notices an area, allows support beneath it, softens effort and observes any change. This is an adaptation, not a lesser completion.

## Mechanism-specific outcome

Primary question:

**“Where is tension now?”**

Options:

- Less in some areas.

- About the same.

- Easier to notice.

- More uncomfortable.

- I stopped or skipped an area.

Total relaxation is not required.

## Visual amendments

Keep the dark plum field, emerald body illumination and local tense/release contrast.

Change:

- replace the fixed idealised body with a neutral, abstract and non-gendered body field;

- offer a labelled body-part list equivalent;

- never imply that the illustration measures the user’s body;

- highlight only the active area and reduce visual noise elsewhere;

- use different form/texture as well as colour for RESTING, GATHERING and RELEASING;

- enlarge safety, skip and current-area controls;

- add a release-only visual in which light disperses without prior compression;

- preserve the same body position and scale across transitions to make contrast legible.

## Memory

With permission, store route length, areas skipped, preferred pace, guidance/audio settings and the user’s optional report of where contrast was easiest to notice.

Do not infer injury, chronic tension, anxiety severity or physiological relaxation from interaction behaviour.

## Handoffs

- Pain or physical discomfort → stop; offer a non-body-based Calm or Ground intervention.

- Breathing discomfort or panic activation → Grounding rather than breath control.

- Restlessness and desire for safe activation → Pulse Shift after its suitability check.

- Cognitive rumination remains primary → Thought or Fact or Then What?.

# Then What?

## Contract

**Primary goal:** Calm  
**Primary design system:** Cognitive Sequence Expansion  
**Intended state:** A catastrophic future image that has collapsed the whole future into one feared moment.  
**Intended change:** Place the feared moment inside one bounded sequence containing response, support, resources and what remains intact.  
**Core mechanism:** Expand a frozen catastrophic frame into a realistic coping timeline without seeking certainty or rehearsing endless worst cases.  
**Signature material:** Luminous suspended frames.  
**Motion verb:** Freeze → widen → return.

## Purpose

Then What? helps someone who is calm enough to reflect continue a feared future image beyond its most catastrophic frame and identify what they could do, who could help and what would remain intact. It is a bounded coping exercise rather than repeated reassurance: the goal is a survivable route through uncertainty, not proof that the feared event will not happen.

## Revised sequence

~~~
HOME + CALM
↓
READINESS AND LOOP CHECK
↓
NAME THE EXACT FEARED MOMENT
↓
FREEZE THE FRAME
↓
WHAT DOES THE MIND SAY THIS WOULD MEAN?
↓
CONTINUE THE FILM ONCE
↓
FIRST MOVE
↓
FIRST PERSON OR SOURCE OF SUPPORT
↓
FIRST RESOURCE
↓
WHAT WOULD REMAIN TRUE OR INTACT
↓
WHAT HAPPENS IN THE NEXT ORDINARY MOMENT
↓
BUILD ONE COPING ROUTE
↓
ACTION NOW · NO ACTION NOW · UNCERTAIN
↓
RETURN TO THE PRESENT
~~~

The continuation occurs once. The app must not produce an open-ended chain of “then what?” questions.

## Readiness and loop check

Ask:

**“Would looking at this future help you plan, or are you already caught in repeated reassurance or worst-case analysis?”**

Routes:

- **Calm enough and planning may help** → continue.

- **Too activated** → Grounding or another regulation route.

- **Already repeating the scenario for certainty** → do not expand it again; route to Urge Surfing, containment or return-to-present support.

- **Immediate danger or current crisis** → leave hypothetical reflection and show relevant support/action options.

## Adaptation levels

### Immediate — One Frame Wider

Name the feared moment, identify one first response and return to the present.

### Standard — Coping Route

Move, person, resource, what remains and next ordinary moment.

### Supported — Guided Timeline

Slower prompts, examples, uncertainty validation and help distinguishing a coping plan from reassurance seeking.

### This is not helping

Collapse the future frames, return to the room and offer Grounding, Urge Surfing or simple containment without asking the user to complete the story.

## Mechanism-specific outcome

Primary question:

**“Is there now a route through the feared moment?”**

Answers:

- Yes, I can see a first response.

- Partly; some of it remains uncertain.

- The issue needs action now.

- No action is needed now.

- Continuing the story is feeding the loop.

- I need support.

## Visual amendments

Keep the luminous frozen frame, horizontal future sequence and gradual widening.

Change:

- cap the timeline at five defined nodes;

- reveal one node at a time rather than presenting an endless road;

- do not make later frames automatically brighter or idyllic;

- represent support, resources and continuity without depicting a guaranteed good outcome;

- make the current/present frame remain faintly visible throughout;

- collapse the timeline immediately when the user exits or reports looping;

- provide a reduced-motion version using a static strip of five frames;

- replace any “route exists” certainty with “a first response exists” when the user only completed part of the path.

## Memory

Optionally save the coping route, not the full catastrophic narrative. The saved card contains:

- feared moment in the user’s words;

- first response;

- support or resource;

- action now / no action now / uncertain.

Do not resurface feared content automatically at bedtime or during unrelated check-ins.

## Handoffs

- High activation → 5–4–3–2–1 Grounding.

- Reassurance or analysis urge → Urge Surfing.

- A specific prediction can be tested safely → Test the Prediction.

- Real task/action required now → Next Easiest Step.

- Safe relational support is needed → Open Channel.

## Evidence-register entries

### PMR

- **Supported claim:** systematic-review evidence suggests PMR can reduce stress and anxiety for some adults.
- **Useful source:** Muhammad Khir et al., PMID 38322293.
- **Important limitation:** studies vary across populations and delivery methods; one session should not promise a specific physiological or psychological result.

### Then What?

- **Supported claim:** the intervention uses established cognitive-therapy principles of decatastrophising, coping appraisal and distinguishing a feared image from a complete future.
- **Evidence wording rule:** make no claim that completing the sequence reduces anxiety or predicts coping success until a suitable source specific to the final mechanism is entered in the evidence register.
