# Elite Standards Pass 01 — Urge Surfing and Test the Prediction

This pass applies the Mentication Elite Intervention Standard to the two interventions with the highest-priority safety sequencing changes. It replaces the relevant board logic before final visual regeneration.

# Urge Surfing

## Contract

**Primary goal:** Calm  
**Primary design system:** Emotional Wave Tracking  
**Intended state:** A strong, non-emergency impulse that is narrowing the user’s available choices.  
**Intended change:** Create a short space between urge and action, whether or not intensity falls.  
**Core mechanism:** Observe the changing sensory experience of an urge, delay automatic action briefly, and choose the next response deliberately.  
**Signature material:** Moonlit water.  
**Motion verb:** Rise → move → pass.

## Purpose

Urge Surfing helps someone notice and stay with a strong but non-emergency urge long enough to regain some choice about what happens next. Rather than fighting the urge or promising that it will disappear, the experience helps the user name it, notice how it changes and protect a brief choice window before deciding what to do. Mindfulness-based urge strategies may change how people respond even when the reported intensity does not immediately decrease.

## Revised sequence

~~~
HOME + CALM
↓
WHAT KIND OF URGE IS THIS?
↓
SAFETY AND SUITABILITY GATE
↓
NAME THE WAVE
↓
FIND THE PULL IN THE BODY OR ENVIRONMENT
↓
CHOOSE AN ANCHOR
↓
CHOOSE A WINDOW
↓
RIDE · TRACK · ADAPT
↓
DO YOU HAVE ANY MORE CHOICE?
↓
WAIT · LEAVE TRIGGER · SUBSTITUTE · REACH OUT · ACT DELIBERATELY
↓
OPTIONALLY SAVE WHAT HELPED
~~~

The safety gate must occur before body tracking, the countdown or immersive wave imagery.

## Safety gate

Ask one direct question:

**“Could acting on this urge put you or someone else in immediate danger?”**

Routes:

- **Yes or unsure** → do not begin the solo surf. Show immediate safety/support options appropriate to the product and region.

- **No** → continue.

The intervention is not used as a solo delay tool for imminent self-harm, overdose, dangerous intoxication, violence, a medical emergency or another time-critical risk.

For eating-disorder, substance-use or compulsive behaviours, the app must not imply that Urge Surfing replaces a treatment or safety plan.

## Adaptation levels

### Immediate — Choice Breath

Approximately 30–45 seconds:

1. Name the urge.

2. Notice one changing sensation.

3. Take one unforced breath.

4. Choose the next safe action.

### Standard — Choice Window

Approximately 90 seconds to three minutes, selected by the user. The duration is a chosen pause, not a claim about how long urges last.

### Supported — Guided Surf

Slower narration, optional body-location prompts, environmental anchoring, intensity check-ins and a wider set of safe next actions.

### This is not helping

Immediately pause motion and offer:

- leave the trigger;

- contact support;

- use Grounding;

- choose a concrete substitute;

- stop without rating the urge.

## Mechanism-specific outcome

Primary question:

**“Do you have any more choice about what happens next?”**

Answers:

- Yes.

- A little.

- Not yet.

- The urge is stronger.

- I need support.

Intensity may be recorded secondarily, but a lower number is not required for completion.

## Visual amendments

Keep the ocean, finger-trace current and before/now wave map.

Change:

- remove “Why 90 seconds?” and any fixed biological claim;

- let the user select a short choice window;

- add a quiet safety gate before the ocean becomes immersive;

- keep Stop and Leave Trigger visible throughout;

- create a static reduced-motion sea state;

- provide an automatic trace for users who cannot or do not want to drag;

- reduce the visual drama if the user reports that the urge is intensifying.

The wave must continue beyond the edge of the screen rather than visibly “vanishing,” avoiding a promise that the urge will end within the session.

## Memory

With permission, store:

- user-named urge category;

- window selected;

- action chosen;

- user-reported choice outcome;

- optional strategy reported as useful.

Do not infer addiction, compulsion type, risk level or therapeutic progress from usage.

## Handoffs

- Immediate danger → safety/support route.

- Too activated to observe → 5–4–3–2–1 Grounding.

- Repeated analysis rather than action urge → Then What? or Thought or Fact depending on content.

- Safe need for connection → Open Channel.

- Safe task avoidance with low activation → Countermove or Next Easiest Step.

# Test the Prediction

## Contract

**Primary goal:** Lift  
**Secondary goal:** Calm  
**Primary design system:** Cognitive Sorting with a real-world behavioural experiment  
**Intended state:** Avoidance or anxious forecasting driven by a specific, testable prediction.  
**Intended change:** Replace an assumed outcome with direct, bounded evidence while preserving safety and ambiguity.  
**Core mechanism:** Define a prediction, design a safe graded experiment, observe what occurs, separate observation from interpretation and update the prediction.  
**Signature material:** Coastal paths and a clear lens.  
**Motion verb:** Forecast → diverge → compare.

## Purpose

Test the Prediction helps someone turn a specific anxious forecast into a small, safe real-world experiment and compare what was predicted with what was directly observed. Behavioural experiments can support learning by testing beliefs through experience, but the result may support, partly support or contradict the original prediction and should never be forced into a positive conclusion.

## Revised sequence

~~~
HOME + LIFT
↓
CAPTURE THE PREDICTION
↓
MAKE IT SPECIFIC AND OBSERVABLE
↓
EXPERIMENT ELIGIBILITY GATE
↓
CHOOSE GENTLE · USEFUL · STRETCH TEST
↓
REVIEW ACTION, PREDICTED OUTCOME AND EVIDENCE
↓
USER CONFIRMS THE PLAN
↓
LEAVE THE APP AND TEST
↓
RETURN · COMPLETED, PARTLY OR NOT COMPLETED
↓
RECORD DIRECT OBSERVATIONS
↓
SEPARATE OBSERVATION FROM INTERPRETATION
↓
COMPARE FORECAST WITH REALITY
↓
UPDATE, KEEP OR SUSPEND THE PREDICTION
↓
SAVE EVIDENCE OR CHOOSE NEXT STEP
~~~

## Experiment eligibility gate

Before generating experiments, check:

1. **Is the prediction specific enough that reality could answer it?**

2. **Can it be tested without meaningful risk, coercion, illegality or violation of another person’s boundaries?**

3. **Is this ordinary uncertainty rather than a belief involving paranoia, delusion, trauma memory or immediate danger?**

4. **Does the user have enough current capacity to do the experiment voluntarily?**

Routes:

- Not specific → return to prediction editing.

- Unsafe or high stakes → do not generate a test; route to appropriate planning, support or professional guidance.

- Requires another person’s compliance → redesign it around the user’s own safe action and observable outcome.

- Too activated → Ground or Calm first.

- Eligible → continue.

Examples the app must not generate include unsafe exposure, medication or medical tests, illegal action, contact with an abusive person, deliberate humiliation, financial risk, deprivation, substance use or violating consent.

## Adaptation levels

### Immediate — Reality Check

For an event that has already occurred:

1. State the prediction.

2. Record one direct observation.

3. Mark what remains interpretation.

4. Keep, soften or suspend the prediction.

### Standard — Graded Experiment

The full forecast, safe experiment, real-world handoff and return sequence.

### Supported — Experiment Builder

Additional prompts to make the prediction measurable, reduce risk, scale the action and identify ambiguous outcomes before the test.

### This is not helping

Offer:

- make the experiment smaller;

- postpone without penalty;

- use Thought or Fact when observation and interpretation are tangled;

- stop if the process is becoming reassurance seeking or compulsive checking.

## Outcome model

The comparison screen supports:

- Prediction not supported by the observations.

- Prediction partly supported.

- Prediction supported.

- Evidence was mixed.

- Test did not answer the question.

- Experiment was not completed.

The app must not automatically declare the feared outcome disproved.

Final update options:

- change the estimated likelihood;

- rewrite the prediction;

- retain it for now;

- mark insufficient evidence;

- identify a practical next action;

- close without saving.

## Visual amendments

Keep the cinematic coastal route, forked paths and forecast/reality lens.

Change:

- place a restrained eligibility screen before path selection;

- give ambiguous evidence a visible third visual state rather than only coral-versus-gold;

- prevent the “reality” path from always appearing brighter or more beautiful than the forecast path;

- keep the user’s original wording visible during comparison;

- visually distinguish observation, interpretation and prediction without relying on colour;

- provide a low-motion static route and native text overlays rather than baked-in labels;

- make the external handoff and saved return point unmistakable.

## Memory

With permission, store:

- prediction;

- user-confirmed experiment;

- predicted likelihood;

- completion status;

- direct observations;

- user-authored interpretation and update.

The Reality Record may summarise user-entered evidence but cannot infer that a belief has been disproved, a fear is irrational or treatment progress has occurred.

## Handoffs

- Observation and interpretation remain tangled → Thought or Fact.

- Experiment cannot be started because the entry step is unclear → Next Easiest Step.

- Avoidance pull remains despite a clear, safe action → Countermove.

- High activation → Grounding or Box Breathing.

- Result reveals a real problem → practical action, support, boundary planning or Open Channel where safe.

## Evidence-register entries

### Urge Surfing

- **Supported claim:** mindfulness-based urge strategies may alter response to cravings even without immediate reductions in reported urge intensity.
- **Useful source:** Bowen & Marlatt, brief urge-surfing study in smokers, PMID 20025372.
- **Important limitation:** evidence is not a universal time course for urges and does not support a fixed 90-second promise.

### Test the Prediction

- **Supported claim:** behavioural experiments are established CBT techniques intended to test predictions through experience.
- **Useful source:** McMillan & Lee systematic review, PMID 20381224.
- **Important limitation:** comparative evidence is methodologically limited and does not justify claiming that every experiment will disconfirm a fear.
