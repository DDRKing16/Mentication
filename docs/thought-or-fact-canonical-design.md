# Thought or Fact — Canonical Intervention Design

Status: proposed canonical design for board amendment and final visual generation.

## Core decision

Retain the premium **Mind Court** world because it gives Thought or Fact a memorable identity, but recast it as a calm evidence review rather than a trial seeking a guilty/not-guilty verdict.

The user remains the authority. The app can organise, prompt and suggest, but it never declares a thought false, diagnoses a distortion, or decides what an event means.

## 1. Purpose

Thought or Fact helps someone slow down a distressing thought and separate what is directly known from what the mind is predicting or interpreting. It is for worry, rumination and self-critical conclusions when the user is settled enough to reflect; by reviewing the available evidence and deciding what remains uncertain or actionable, the user can respond with more perspective rather than treating the first thought as established fact. Thoughts can feel true without being complete accounts of reality, and examining evidence can support more flexible, balanced thinking.

## 2. Canonical user flow

HOME + CALM

↓

READINESS CHECK

↓

OPEN A CASE

↓

CAPTURE THE EXACT THOUGHT

↓

RATE HOW CONVINCING IT FEELS

↓

AI SUGGESTS POSSIBLE CLAIM PARTS

↓

USER CONFIRMS, EDITS OR REJECTS THEM

↓

SORT INTO FACT · INTERPRETATION · PREDICTION

↓

REVIEW SUPPORTING, MISSING AND ALTERNATIVE EVIDENCE

↓

CHOOSE A WORKING RULING

↓

RE-RATE · CHOOSE NEXT STEP · OPTIONALLY SAVE

### Readiness routes

- **Too activated to reflect** → 5–4–3–2–1 Grounding or Box Breathing.

- **Urge to keep analysing/reassurance seeking** → Urge Surfing or Then What?, depending on the pattern.

- **Immediate danger or urgent support need** → leave the reflective flow and show appropriate support options.

### Working ruling options

- **Supported by what I know** → identify the real problem, boundary or next action.

- **Partly supported** → state what is known, what is being added and what remains uncertain.

- **Mostly interpretation or prediction** → create a balanced response or route to Test the Prediction.

- **Not enough information** → tolerate uncertainty, choose whether any action is needed, and close the review.

### Valid completion outcomes

- I see the thought differently.

- It still feels convincing, but I can see what is uncertain.

- The concern is real and needs action.

- I am too activated to continue.

- Reviewing it is feeding the loop, so I am stopping.

## 3. Visual and audio direction

### Visual concept

Use a refined, low-lit **private evidence chamber** rather than a theatrical courtroom. Preserve oxblood leather, parchment, dark timber, warm brass and balanced scales, but remove punitive or finalising imagery.

The visual journey moves from one closed case file into a calm evidence table:

1. **Closed file:** one thought, written exactly as experienced.

2. **Claim markers:** editable slips identify possible facts, interpretations and predictions.

3. **Evidence table:** the three categories occupy distinct, equally weighted areas; nothing is automatically placed on the scales.

4. **Review lens:** prompts reveal missing information and alternative explanations without arguing with the user.

5. **Working ruling:** a balanced card remains editable and explicitly provisional.

### Language changes

- “Put a thought on trial” → **Open a case**

- “Charge sheet” → **What the thought is claiming**

- “Potential distortions” → **Possible thinking patterns**

- “Cross-examination” → **Review questions**

- “Verdict” → **Working ruling**

- “Case closed” → **Review saved** or **Review complete**

- “Juror belief” → **How convincing it feels**

The intervention name remains **Thought or Fact**. “Mind Court” can remain the visual-world subtitle, not the clinical mechanism or a claim of objective judgment.

### Motion

Slow file opening, cards sliding into user-selected positions, gentle scale movement only after the user sorts evidence, and a quiet widening of the room at completion. No gavel, stamping, dramatic reveal or guilty/not-guilty animation.

Provide a reduced-motion mode using simple fades and static cards.

### Sound

Low room tone, subtle paper and brass sounds, restrained haptics at confirmed user actions, and warm unhurried narration. No gavel, courtroom crowd, suspense music or triumphant resolution.

Audio, haptics and narration must each be independently switchable.

## 4. Mentication upgrade

### Guided claim parsing

The app can suggest which parts of the sentence appear to be observations, interpretations or predictions. Suggestions arrive as editable hypotheses—not answers—and remain unclassified until the user confirms them.

### Evidence without forced positivity

The review makes room for evidence that supports the concern, contradicts it, is ambiguous or is still missing. A real problem routes to action rather than being reframed away.

### State-aware handoffs

The experience detects user-reported activation, analysis loops and actionable concerns, then offers a better-fit intervention without making the user restart their journey.

### Reusable clarity card

The result can be saved as:

**What I know:** …

**What my mind is adding:** …

**What remains uncertain:** …

**What I can do next:** …

### Pattern memory with permission

With explicit consent, the app may remember user-confirmed patterns and useful review questions. It must never state that it has detected a distortion as fact. The user can inspect, correct and delete saved learning.

## AI boundaries

- Do not determine whether the whole thought is true or false.

- Do not invent facts, evidence, intentions or alternative explanations.

- Do not label a person, relationship or mental-health condition.

- Do not encourage repeated checking or reassurance seeking.

- Do not use this flow to challenge delusional/paranoid beliefs, trauma memories, abuse disclosures or immediate safety concerns.

- Always provide edit, reject, skip and stop options.

## Final asset brief

Retain from the existing Mind Court production concept:

- oxblood, parchment, brass and near-black palette;

- private case-file opening;

- premium evidence table and scales;

- strong typographic hierarchy.

Replace or regenerate:

- trial, charge, cross-examination, juror and verdict screens;

- case-closed stamp;

- any visual that implies the app is the judge;

- baked-in screen text and controls.

Required component-ready exports:

- chamber background in dark and higher-contrast variants;

- closed/open case-file layers;

- blank parchment card states;

- fact, interpretation, prediction and uncertainty markers;

- balanced-scale foreground layers and static reduced-motion version;

- subtle paper/brass sound set;

- no-text hero artwork for the intervention library.
